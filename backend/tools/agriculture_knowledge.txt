AGROAGENT – GENERAL AGRICULTURE KNOWLEDGE BASE

============================================================
PURPOSE
============================================================

This document is the general agriculture knowledge source for AgroAgent.

AgroAgent should use this knowledge base to answer general questions about:

- crops
- crop cultivation
- sowing
- planting
- transplanting
- soil
- soil pH
- NPK
- fertilizers
- irrigation
- rainfall
- weather effects on crops
- weeds
- pests
- diseases
- plant symptoms
- crop growth stages
- harvesting
- post-harvest handling
- storage
- crop rotation
- organic farming
- farm management

For current weather or farm-specific measurements, use the appropriate
specialized tool instead of inventing values.

use the crop recommendation model.

============================================================
1. GENERAL CROP CULTIVATION
============================================================

General crop cultivation normally follows these steps:

1. Select a crop suitable for the region and season.
2. Select a suitable variety.
3. Check soil characteristics.
4. Conduct a soil test when possible.
5. Prepare the field.
6. Select healthy seed or planting material.
7. Treat seed when recommended.
8. Sow or transplant at the appropriate time.
9. Maintain suitable plant spacing.
10. Provide appropriate irrigation.
11. Apply nutrients according to soil and crop requirements.
12. Control weeds.
13. Monitor pests and diseases.
14. Monitor crop growth.
15. Take corrective action when problems are identified.
16. Harvest at suitable maturity.
17. Clean, grade and store the produce properly.

============================================================
2. CROP SELECTION
============================================================

Crop selection can depend on:

- climate
- temperature
- rainfall
- soil type
- soil pH
- water availability
- growing season
- crop duration
- market demand
- farmer resources
- pest and disease risk

A crop that performs well in one region may not perform equally well
in another region.

When recommending a crop using AgroAgent's crop model, measured soil
and weather information should be used rather than invented values.

============================================================
3. SOIL
============================================================

Soil provides:

- physical support
- water
- air
- nutrients
- a root environment

Important soil properties include:

- soil texture
- soil structure
- soil pH
- organic matter
- drainage
- water-holding capacity
- nutrient availability

Common soil texture categories include:

- sandy soil
- clay soil
- loamy soil

Loamy soil is often suitable for many crops because it can provide
a balance of drainage and water retention, but crop suitability depends
on the particular crop and local conditions.

============================================================
4. SOIL pH
============================================================

Soil pH affects nutrient availability.

Different crops have different preferred pH ranges.

Very acidic or very alkaline soil can affect the availability of nutrients
and may reduce crop growth.

General steps:

1. Test soil pH.
2. Identify the crop's preferred range.
3. Identify whether the soil is acidic or alkaline.
4. Use appropriate soil amendments when required.
5. Re-test soil when appropriate.

Do not recommend a specific amendment quantity without appropriate
soil-test information.

============================================================
5. NITROGEN
============================================================

Nitrogen is an essential plant nutrient.

Nitrogen supports:

- leaf growth
- vegetative growth
- chlorophyll production
- overall plant development

Possible nitrogen deficiency symptoms can include:

- older leaves becoming yellow
- slow growth
- small plants
- reduced vegetative development

However, yellow leaves can have many causes and should not automatically
be diagnosed as nitrogen deficiency.

============================================================
6. PHOSPHORUS
============================================================

Phosphorus is important for:

- root development
- energy transfer
- reproductive growth
- flowering
- seed development

Phosphorus availability is affected by soil conditions, including pH.

Fertilizer decisions should consider soil-test results and crop requirements.

============================================================
7. POTASSIUM
============================================================

Potassium supports:

- water regulation
- plant strength
- enzyme activity
- stress tolerance
- crop quality

Potassium deficiency can cause symptoms such as leaf-edge discoloration
or scorching in some crops, but diagnosis should consider the whole plant
and soil conditions.

============================================================
8. NPK
============================================================

NPK refers to:

N = Nitrogen
P = Phosphorus
K = Potassium

N generally supports vegetative growth.

P supports root and reproductive development.

K supports water regulation, plant strength and physiological processes.

The appropriate NPK requirement depends on:

- crop
- soil
- growth stage
- expected production
- nutrient availability

Soil testing should be used when possible before major fertilizer decisions.

============================================================
9. ORGANIC MATTER
============================================================

Organic matter can improve:

- soil structure
- water retention
- nutrient cycling
- microbial activity

Sources can include:

- compost
- farmyard manure
- properly managed crop residues
- other suitable organic materials

Organic materials should be properly managed before application.

============================================================
10. FIELD PREPARATION
============================================================

General field preparation may include:

1. Remove unwanted vegetation where appropriate.
2. Prepare the soil according to crop requirements.
3. Improve drainage when necessary.
4. Incorporate suitable organic matter when appropriate.
5. Prepare a suitable seedbed.
6. Ensure appropriate soil moisture before sowing.

Field preparation depends on the crop, soil and farming system.

============================================================
11. SEED SELECTION
============================================================

Good seed should be:

- healthy
- viable
- suitable for the crop
- suitable for the region
- suitable for the season
- free from obvious damage

Poor-quality seed can lead to:

- poor germination
- weak seedlings
- uneven crop establishment
- lower crop performance

============================================================
12. SEED GERMINATION
============================================================

Germination requires:

- viable seed
- suitable moisture
- suitable temperature
- oxygen

Poor germination can result from:

- poor seed quality
- excessive water
- insufficient moisture
- unsuitable temperature
- disease
- damaged seed

============================================================
13. SOWING
============================================================

Important sowing factors include:

- sowing time
- seed quality
- sowing depth
- plant spacing
- soil moisture
- soil condition
- weather

Seeds should not be planted too deeply or too shallowly for the crop.

============================================================
14. TRANSPLANTING
============================================================

General transplanting steps:

1. Produce healthy seedlings.
2. Harden seedlings when appropriate.
3. Prepare the field.
4. Transplant at a suitable growth stage.
5. Maintain suitable spacing.
6. Provide appropriate moisture after transplanting.
7. Protect young plants from severe environmental stress.

Avoid unnecessary root damage during transplanting.

============================================================
15. IRRIGATION
============================================================

Irrigation decisions should consider:

- crop
- crop growth stage
- soil type
- soil moisture
- recent rainfall
- rainfall forecast
- temperature
- drainage
- water availability

Avoid unnecessary irrigation when substantial rainfall is expected.

Excessive irrigation can cause:

- waterlogging
- root damage
- nutrient loss
- oxygen deficiency
- increased disease risk

Insufficient irrigation can cause:

- wilting
- slow growth
- flower or fruit loss
- reduced yield

============================================================
16. RAINFALL AND WEATHER
============================================================

Weather can influence:

- crop growth
- irrigation
- disease development
- pest activity
- flowering
- fruit development
- harvesting
- field operations

Rainfall can reduce the immediate need for irrigation.

Heavy rainfall can cause:

- waterlogging
- soil erosion
- nutrient loss
- crop damage
- disease-favorable conditions

High humidity can favor some diseases.

Temperature affects crop growth and development.

For current weather questions, AgroAgent should use the weather tool
when farm coordinates are available.

============================================================
17. WATERLOGGING
============================================================

Waterlogging occurs when excess water remains in the root zone.

Possible effects include:

- reduced oxygen around roots
- root damage
- poor nutrient uptake
- increased disease risk
- reduced plant growth

Management can include:

- improving drainage
- avoiding unnecessary irrigation
- maintaining suitable field channels
- considering soil and field conditions

============================================================
18. DROUGHT AND WATER STRESS
============================================================

Water stress can cause:

- wilting
- slow growth
- leaf rolling in some crops
- flower loss
- fruit loss
- reduced yield

Management can include:

- efficient irrigation
- mulching
- appropriate planting time
- suitable crop selection
- suitable varieties
- improving soil organic matter
- reducing unnecessary water loss

============================================================
19. FERTILIZER MANAGEMENT
============================================================

Fertilizer management should consider:

- soil-test results
- crop
- crop growth stage
- nutrient requirement
- existing soil fertility
- expected production

General principles:

1. Test soil when possible.
2. Identify nutrient requirements.
3. Apply suitable nutrients.
4. Avoid excessive application.
5. Apply nutrients at suitable crop stages.
6. Monitor crop response.

Do not assume every plant problem is caused by fertilizer deficiency.

============================================================
20. WEED MANAGEMENT
============================================================

Weeds compete with crops for:

- water
- nutrients
- sunlight
- space

Weed-management methods include:

- hand weeding
- mechanical control
- mulching
- crop rotation
- suitable herbicides

Herbicide selection depends on:

- crop
- weed species
- product
- crop growth stage

Never invent herbicide doses.

============================================================
21. INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT
============================================================

Integrated Pest Management combines multiple approaches.

General steps:

1. Inspect the crop regularly.
2. Identify the pest.
3. Monitor pest population.
4. Maintain field sanitation.
5. Use healthy planting material.
6. Encourage beneficial organisms.
7. Use physical control when practical.
8. Use biological control when suitable.
9. Use pesticides only when justified.
10. Follow the product label and local agricultural guidance.

26. TOMATO
============================================================

Tomato is a warm-season vegetable crop.

General cultivation steps:

1. Select a suitable variety.
2. Prepare well-drained soil.
3. Use healthy seed or seedlings.
4. Transplant at a suitable stage when using seedlings.
5. Maintain suitable plant spacing.
6. Irrigate according to soil moisture.
7. Control weeds.
8. Apply nutrients according to crop and soil requirements.
9. Provide support when required.
10. Monitor pests and diseases.
11. Harvest mature fruits.

Common problems include:

- early blight
- late blight
- insect pests
- nutrient deficiencies
- water stress

TOMATO EARLY BLIGHT:

Early blight is a fungal disease that can affect tomato leaves.

Symptoms can include brown spots and concentric ring patterns.

Good field sanitation and removal of severely affected plant material
can help reduce disease spread.

TOMATO LATE BLIGHT:

Late blight can cause dark lesions on leaves and stems.

Cool and humid conditions can favor disease development.

Affected plants should be monitored carefully.

39. CROP ROTATION
============================================================

Crop rotation means growing different crops in sequence on the same field.

Potential benefits include:

- improved nutrient management
- disruption of some pest cycles
- disruption of some disease cycles
- improved weed management
- improved soil health

Rotation should be designed according to local conditions and crop needs.

============================================================
40. MULCHING
============================================================

Mulching means covering soil around crops with suitable material.

Potential benefits include:

- reduced evaporation
- reduced weed growth
- reduced soil temperature fluctuations
- reduced erosion
- improved soil organic matter when organic mulch decomposes

54. NO INVENTION RULE
============================================================

Never invent:

- soil values
- NPK measurements
- soil pH
- temperature
- humidity
- rainfall
- coordinates
- soil moisture
- disease diagnosis
- fertilizer dose
- pesticide dose
- herbicide dose
- yield
- market price

If required information is missing, ask the farmer only for the information
needed to answer the question.

For general educational questions, provide general agricultural guidance.

For farm-specific or high-impact treatment decisions, explain uncertainty
and recommend checking appropriate local agricultural guidance or the
product label.
